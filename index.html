Each part of the page is on one grid

This page uses one grid for all of its parts. The unit of the grid is the size of the text. A column is three units wide, and a gutter, the distance between two columns, is two units wide. The baseline is the bottom of letters such as H and x. A line is the distance from one baseline to the next, one and a half units. The baselines of the text are on the grid lines, which are one line apart. The text width, the width of the paragraphs, is seven columns, and the five columns on the right hold pictures and notes. When the size of the text or the line height changes, all distances on the page change with it. No distance on the page comes from the eye.

A picture is on the same grid. The cap line is the top of the capital letters. The width of a picture is a number of columns. Its top edge is at the cap line of the text beside it, and its bottom edge is on a grid line. Its height is the height of a capital letter plus whole lines, as near to its ratio as possible. The image is cut to that height, so it does not stretch. Thus the text after a picture starts on a grid line again.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the headings and the text

A heading is larger than the text, and its line height is a number of half baselines. Thus each line of a heading is a whole number of half baselines below the line before it. The margin above a heading is larger than the margin below it, so the heading belongs to the text after it. The two margins are near three and five half baselines, two numbers of the Fibonacci series. The first baseline of the text after a heading is always on a grid line.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the smallest heading and the paragraph below it

The smallest heading has the size of the text and a bold weight. Its line height is one line, so each of its lines is on a grid line. Its margins above and below have the same ratio as for the larger headings. The first baseline of the paragraph after it is two lines below its last baseline. Thus the smallest heading uses the grid of the text with no change.

Pictures on the left, text on the right

Here the text is in the columns on the right. The pictures are in the text width, the seven columns on the left. The pictures and the heading start at the same cap line.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the pictures inside a paragraph

A picture can also be inside a paragraph, with the text around it. Its width is a number of columns plus one unit of the gutter. Thus a margin of one unit is between the picture and the text, and the text beside it starts at a column edge. The top edge of the picture is at the cap line of the first line of text, and its bottom edge is on a baseline. The text beside the picture is narrower, and the text below the picture has its full width again. Here the picture is square and two columns wide.

A small picture follows the same rules as a large picture. This picture is one column wide, plus one unit of the gutter. The column sets its width, and the lines set its height, so no part of its size comes from the eye. Its top edge is at the cap line of the first line, and its bottom edge is on a baseline. A picture of this size is near the size of a letter, for example an initial at the start of a paragraph. After the picture, the text continues with its full width.

A portrait picture is taller than it is wide. At three by four on two columns, it is taller than the square above it, so more lines of text are narrow. The height of the picture is a whole number of lines, so the picture does not stretch and the text does not stop for it. Where the picture ends, the next line of text has its full width again. The eye then finds the left edge of the column at the same position as before. A tall picture needs a long paragraph, because with a short paragraph the picture extends below the end of the text.

A landscape picture on three columns leaves four columns for the text beside it. Four columns hold only a few words in each line, so this width is good for a few lines only. At thirteen by eight the picture is low, and the text soon has its full width again. Thirteen to eight is near the golden section, a ratio that the page also uses for the sizes of its headings. The width of the picture is three columns plus one unit of the gutter. The picture and the text use the same edges.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the rules

A rule divides the text and adds no picture. It belongs to the text before it, as a heading belongs to the text after it. Thus the ratio of the margins is the other way round: two half baselines above the rule, and near three below it. The rule is centred on a grid line, and the margin below it is never smaller than the margin above it. The length of the rule is a number of columns, and here it is the text width.

Below the rule, the text continues on the grid. The rule is an eighth of a unit thick and has the colour of the text. Thus it shows a pause between two thoughts, not the start of a new section.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the colour areas

A colour area is a picture with no content. Its width is a number of columns, and its top edge is at the cap line of the text beside it. Its bottom edge is on a grid line, as near to its ratio as possible. A colour area gives weight to a part of the page, shows where a section starts, or holds a row together. It uses the two colours of the page: the red of the rule below the title, and the black of the text.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the boxes

A box separates a piece of text from the paragraphs around it, for example a note, a definition or an example. A box does not have its own grid. Its background is a number of columns wide, as a picture is. The text in the box has the same size and line height as each other paragraph.

A box with its own heading

The background of the box goes from one column edge to another. The padding at the sides of the box is one column on the right and one unit less on the left, so the text does not touch the edge. The background starts two lines above the baseline of the heading and stops one line below the last baseline. A box with no heading has two lines above its text. Below its text, it has at least one and a half lines, up to the next grid line.

The margin around each box is the same. The background starts one line below the last baseline of the text above it. The next baseline after the box is two lines below the background. In the box, the text can stop between two grid lines. Then the background adds half a baseline at its bottom, and the text after the box is on the grid lines again.

Between two boxes, the text continues as usual. Each box has its own margin above and below it, so two boxes do not touch. The paragraphs between the boxes are part of the body text.

This box is red and has no heading. Its text has the colour of the page. The line below a link in this box, for example to the links, also has the colour of the page. The background starts two lines above the first baseline and stops two lines below the last baseline. Thus the text stays on the grid lines, and the margin around the box is the same as around each other box.

After the red box, the page continues with plain text. Its first baseline is on a grid line, as after each other part of the page.

A box in black

This box starts with a smaller heading, and its text is near the heading. The first baseline of the text is one and a half lines below the heading, so the text is between two grid lines. The background then adds half a baseline at its bottom. Thus the margin around the box is the same as around each other box.

After the black box, the text continues, and its first baseline is on a grid line again. The parts around the box need no information about the box. The margin above the box comes from the paragraph above it, and the box keeps its own padding inside. Below the box, the next paragraph gets the same margin as after each other box. For the reader, the box is a pause, not a break, and the columns and the lines continue after it.

Each part of the page is on one grid: a box directly after a heading

This box comes directly after a heading, with no paragraph between them. The background starts where the text after the heading would start. The text in the box keeps its own padding. After the box, the page is on its grid lines again.

Directly after a heading, the background starts near the cap line that a paragraph would have there. Its position moves in steps of half a baseline. Thus the margin between the heading and the background is the margin below the heading. The background can then start between two grid lines. Then it adds half a baseline at its bottom, so that it stops on a grid line.

A box inside a text block

Here the box is not a cell of the page grid. It is part of a text block: a heading and its text in one cell of the grid.

This box is inside the text block, between two of its paragraphs. Around the background, the box has padding, not a margin. Thus the padding adds to the margin below the paragraph above it, and the two do not merge. The box has the same margin and padding here as in the page grid.

This paragraph comes after the box and is also inside the text block.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the margin notes

A margin note is in the columns beside the text, where a picture can also be. It has the size and the line height of the text. Thus each of its lines is on a baseline of the paragraph beside it. Only its italic style and its light weight make it different, as for a caption. Its first line is on the first line of the paragraph, as a picture beside a paragraph starts at its cap line.

This note is three columns wide. Its lines are on the baselines of the text.

Below the note is the same margin as below a paragraph. When the note is longer than its paragraph, the next paragraph starts one empty line below the note. Thus the page stays on its grid lines.

A note can also refer to one word lower in a paragraph. The note is then in the HTML of the paragraph, directly after that word11 This note refers to the word with the number 1. It starts on the line of that word., and the browser moves it into the columns beside the text. There it starts on the line of that word. When the line breaks of the text change, the word goes to another line, and the note goes with it. A note of this type is not part of the rows of the grid. When it is longer than its paragraph, the text below does not move down for it.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the pull quotes

A pull quote shows a few words from the text again, in a larger size. It is between two paragraphs, for a reader who only looks quickly through the page. Its size and line height follow the rules of a heading, so its line height is a number of half baselines. It has the text width.

A pull quote shows a few words from the text again, in a larger size.

A heading belongs to the text after it, but a pull quote belongs to neither paragraph. Thus the margin above it and the margin below it are the same: one empty line, as between two paragraphs. The margin above is a little larger, so that the paragraph after the quote is on a grid line again. The quote is italic and has the light weight of the text, so it shows as a quote and not as a heading. Its opening quotation mark is in the page margin, to the left of the edge of the text. A class can make its first letter bold, as in the second quote below.

A pull quote can also move out of the columns of the text, by whole columns to the left or to the right. It keeps its width and the steps of the column grid. But its edges are not in line with the paragraphs around it, so the reader sees it as separate from them.

The quote moves past the edge of the type area into the page margin.

The type area is the area of the twelve columns. Here the text starts in the first column of the type area. The quote moves past the edge of the type area into the page margin. It moves by one column and one gutter, as if the grid continued past its edge. A quote that moves to the right goes into the columns beside the text. In both cases, the margin above and below the quote is the same as for a quote in the columns of the text.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the lists

A list is a set of items, for example parts or steps. Each item starts on a new line, with a mark in front of it. The lines of a list are one line apart, as the lines of a paragraph are. Thus each baseline of a list is on a grid line. Above and below a list is one empty line, as between two paragraphs.

  • A column is three units wide.
  • A gutter is two units wide.
  • A line is one and a half units.
  • The text width is seven columns, and the five columns on the right hold pictures, notes and quotes that move to the right.

The marks are to the left of the edge of the text, in an area as wide as a gutter. Thus the words of the items are in line with the words of the paragraphs. Between a mark and the first word is a distance of half a unit. When an item has more than one line, its next line starts at the edge of the text, below the first word of the item.

  2. Set the line height.

A numbered list shows steps in a sequence. The numbers are aligned on the right, so their full stops are in a vertical line. The numbers are bold, so each step is easy to find.

Lists in a box

  • In a box, the marks do not go into the padding. They start at the edge of the text, in line with the heading.
  • The words of the items start one and a half units to the right of the edge of the text.
  • The lines of the items are on the grid lines, as outside the box.
  1. Put the heading at the top of the box.
  2. Put the lists below the heading, with one empty line between them.
  3. Stop the background one line below the last baseline, or one and a half lines if the last baseline is between two grid lines.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the tables

A table shows values in rows and columns. Each row of a table is a line of text, so its baselines are on the grid lines. The columns of the table are on the columns of the page. Thus each column of the table starts at a column edge, and a gutter is between two columns of the table.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the links

A link takes the reader to another part of the page or to another page. A link has the size, the weight and the line height of the text, so it does not move a baseline. A red line below the words shows the link, for example to the boxes or to the tables above. The line is as thick as a thin rule, and its distance below the baseline is the same as its thickness. Where a letter goes below the baseline, the line has a gap.

When the pointer is on a link, the words of the link are red. When the link has the focus, a red outline is around it. In a red box or a black box, the line below a link has the colour of the text of the box. A link that the reader used before looks the same as the other links.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the code

Code is text for a computer, for example the name of a property such as text-box or of a class such as .paragraph. In a paragraph, code has a monospace font, in which each letter has the same width. The capital letters of this font have the same height as the capital letters of the text. Thus the code stays on the baseline, and the line height does not change.

.paragraph {
  text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic;
  margin: 0 0 var(--paragraph-margin);
}

A block of code has one line of code on each grid line. Its lines are one line apart, as the lines of a paragraph are, and above and below the block is one empty line. The block keeps the spaces at the start of each line, so the structure of the code stays clear. A line of code that is longer than the text width continues on the next line.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the icons

An icon is a small picture with the meaning of a word, for example an arrow. An icon is one unit wide and one unit high, as large as the text. Its middle is at the middle of the capital letters, as for a checkbox, so an icon in a line does not change the line height. The lines of an icon are an eighth of a unit thick, as a thin rule is. Between an icon and the word after it is a distance of half a unit.

An arrow in front of a link shows where the link goes. For example, the form elements are below this section. The icon has the colour of the text, so it becomes red with the words of the link when the pointer is on it.

In a button, the icon is in front of the word, at the same distance. The icon and the word together are in the middle of the button.

Each part of the page is on one grid: the form elements

A field is an area where the reader enters or selects a value. A text field is a grey field for one line of text. Its label is above the field. The text in the field is on a grid line, two lines below the baseline of the label. Thus a form keeps the grid lines of the page and of each column beside it.

A text field is two and a half lines deep, and the baseline of its text is on a grid line. The label is half a baseline above the field, and a description or an error message can be between them. Fields can be side by side, with their labels on one baseline and the same edges. A select and a text area use the same rules as a text field.

Checkboxes and radio buttons are lines of text, one line apart, with a square or a circle in front of each line. Many choices can be in columns of two or more grid columns, row by row, and the choices of one row start on the same baseline. A checked choice is red, as a field in focus is. From label to label, a group of choices has the same lines as a text field.

The submit button is red and as deep as a text field, and a second button can be beside it in grey. A field with an error has a red message above it and a red rule to its left. A disabled field, choice or button has no fill, a thin grey outline and grey text. An optional field has the word optional after its label, in the style of a description.

In a box, the fields, the squares and circles of the choices, and the second button have the colour of the page. Thus they show as openings in the grey. The padding at the sides is the same as in each other box: one column on the right and one unit less on the left. A field has a hard edge, and a narrower band of grey beside it looks like a thin frame.

Below its last text field or button, the box adds two lines of grey. Below its last choice, it adds one and a half lines. Thus the padding below is never less than the padding above the first line of the box. The bottom edge of the box stays on a grid line.
